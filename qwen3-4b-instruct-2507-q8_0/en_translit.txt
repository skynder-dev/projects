Title: Why Schools Buy Laptops Twice as Much as in Stores
Format: vertical
Narrator: Maya (woman, narrator)
Principal Tom (man, school principal)
Tech Buyer Sue (woman, procurement officer)
Lenovo Corp (company, tech supplier)
David (man, student parent)

== School | title: Why Schools Buy Laptops Twice as Much as in Stores / a hidden markup
Maya (point up, sly): A school buys laptops twice as much as at a store. Why?
Maya (explain, neutral): Let’s see how the numbers hide a secret.

== Office | compare: Laptop in store $800 vs laptop in school $1,600
Sue (point, worried): In the store, a laptop costs eight hundred dollars.
Sue (think, surprised): But at the school, we pay one thousand six hundred.

== Board | flow: School budget -> Lenovo $1.6M; Lenovo -> Retail price $800; Lenovo -> Profit $800K
Maya: The school pays one point six million for fifty laptops.
Maya: Each costs only eight hundred in stores.
Maya: That’s a profit of eight hundred thousand — all from the school’s budget.

== Shop | bag: "Laptops for students"
David (wave, happy): My son gets a new laptop. He's excited!
David (rub hands, happy): But I checked — same model, but twice the price.

== Office | tender: TENDER; Must be “education-grade” brand; Minimum five-year warranty; Must include software suite
Sue (talk, sly): We only accept bids from approved suppliers.
Sue (point up, happy): Education-grade, five years, full software.
Tech Buyer Sue (facepalm, sad) [bubble]: Why can’t we just buy from the store?

== Night | magnifier: "Education-grade"
Maya (magnifier, sly): “Education-grade” isn’t a real standard.
Maya: It’s just a label. A way to charge more.

== Board | bars: Store price 800, school price 1600, markup 100%
Maya: The store sells at $800. The school pays $1,600.
Maya: That’s a hundred percent markup — hidden in the rules.

== City hall | timeline: June – tender issued; July – only one bid from Lenovo; August – contract approved
Maya (timeline, neutral): June: tender opens.
Maya: July: only Lenovo responds.
Maya: August: contract approved. No competition.

== Home | counter: $800 → $1,600 (50 laptops)
David (think, worried): Fifty laptops at $1,600? That’s eight hundred thousand dollars.
David (point, angry): My tax dollars pay for a school that buys twice as much.

== Board | list: Red flags; Only one supplier; No price comparison; “Education-grade” label; Software included
Maya (explain, sly): How to spot it?
Maya (point): One supplier. No price check.
Maya (point up): And a fake label like “education-grade.”

== Narrator | final scene
Maya (wave, happy): Schools don’t need twice the price.
Maya (firm, clear): Always compare prices. Always ask: Who wrote the rules?
Maya (hands up, calm): When you see a price double — check the contract. The school pays. The supplier profits. And the public loses.

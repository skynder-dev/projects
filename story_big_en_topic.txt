== Hospital | title: Why a City Hospital / Pays Three Times More for the Same Medicine
Dana (wave, neutral): A hospital needs medicine. It should be simple, right?
Dana (explain, sly): Let's see how it ends up costing three times more.

== Office | tender: TENDER; 5 years of experience; special delivery; 3 days to apply
Dana: The hospital announces a tender for a new medicine. A fair price is about two hundred thousand dollars per bottle.
Paul (present, sly): Perfectly normal requirements!

== Shop | compare: Generic drug $200 vs Brand name drug $600
Sally (worried, sad) [bubble]: Why can’t we use the cheaper one?
Dana: Paul writes the tender for only one supplier. Five years of experience. A special delivery. And just three days to apply.

== Office | contract: CONTRACT; price $600 000; stamp APPROVED
Paul (point up, happy): Approved! Our hospital deserves the best!
Dr. Helen (nod, neutral): We need this medicine. No time to waste.

== Board | flow: Hospital budget -> Larry $600K; Larry -> Medicine $200K; Larry -> Kickback
Dana: The hospital pays Larry six hundred thousand dollars for one hundred bottles.
Dana: Larry buys the medicine for two hundred thousand. And the difference goes somewhere else.

== Hospital | bag: $400 000
Larry (rubs hands, sly) [bubble]: A nice profit. For everyone involved.
Mark (scratches head, worried): This seems... odd.

== Board | percent: 200% kickback
Dana: Larry keeps two hundred thousand for himself. And another two hundred goes to Paul and others who helped.
Dana: That’s a 200% kickback. On the same medicine.

== Hospital | magnifier: invoice
Mark (show, worried): The invoice says this is brand name. But I checked. It's generic.
Sally (facepalm, sad): We’re paying three times more for the same thing.

== Board | bars: fair price 200, paid 600; kickback 400
Dana: One medicine. Paid three times the fair price.
Dana: And every dollar of it came from patients and insurance.

== City hall | list: Red flags; Only one bidder; Rushed deadlines; Price far above the market
Mark (explain, angry): How to spot it?
Mark (point): One bidder and rushed deadlines.
Mark (point up): And a price far above the market.
Dana (wave, happy): Follow the money. And ask who wrote the tender.

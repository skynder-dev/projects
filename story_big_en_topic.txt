== Hospital | title: Why a City Hospital / Pays Three Times More for the Same Medicine
Dana (wave, neutral): A hospital needs medicine. Simple, right?
Dana (explain, sly): Let's see how it ends up costing three times more.

== Office | tender: TENDER; 5 years of experience; local supplier only; 3 days to apply
Dana: The hospital posts a tender for medicine. A fair price is about one point two million dollars.
Mr. Price (present, sly): We need a local supplier. It's better for the community.

== Shop | compare: Brand X $120 / bottle vs Brand Y $45 / bottle
Sally (think, worried) [bubble]: Why is Brand X so much more expensive?
Dana: Larry, the only supplier, offers Brand X at three times the price of Brand Y.

== Office | contract: CONTRACT; price $3 600 000; stamp APPROVED
Mr. Price (point up, happy): Approved! It’s the best for our patients.
Dr. Elaine (cross, worried) [bubble]: This is not right.

== Board | flow: Hospital budget -> Larry $3.6M; Larry -> Medicine $1.2M; Larry -> Officials kickback
Dana: The hospital pays Larry three point six million.
Dana: Larry spends one point two million on the medicine.
Dana: And the rest quietly goes back to the officials who signed.

== Hospital | bag: $2.4M
Larry (rubs hands, sly) [bubble]: That’s a nice profit.
Tom (magnifier, worried) [bubble]: Where did this money go?

== Board | percent: 60% kickback
Dana: Larry keeps sixty percent of the money as a kickback.
Tom (facepalm, sad): This is how the system works.

== Office | list: Red flags; Only one supplier; Rushed deadlines; Price far above the market
Sally (explain, angry): How to spot it?
Sally (point): Only one supplier and rushed deadlines.
Sally (point up): And a price way above the market.

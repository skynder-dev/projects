== Hospital | title: Why a City Hospital Pays Three Times More / for the Same Medicine
Dana (point up, surprised): A single dose of insulin costs more than it should.
Dana (explain, sly): Let’s see how the price gets tripled — and who ends up paying.

== Office | contract: CONTRACT; price $120 000; stamp APPROVED; for 1000 vials per month
Dr. Elaine (rubbing hands, happy): We signed a deal for insulin. $120,000 a month.
Mr. Frank (think, worried): That’s three times what we paid last year.
Dana: Last year it was $40,000. This year? $120,000.

== Shop | tender: TENDER; must be "premium" brand; delivered in 3 days; only one pharmacy allowed
Mr. Frank (point, sly): The tender says only Miles Co. can supply.
Dr. Lee (present, neutral): They claim their insulin is “clinically superior.”
Dana: But the medicine is the same. Just labeled differently.

== Board | compare: Standard insulin $40k vs Premium brand $120k
Mr. Frank (facepalm, angry) [bubble]: Same dose. Same effect. Three times more.
Dana: The price difference comes from branding — not quality.

== Office | flow: Hospital budget -> Miles Co. $120K; Miles Co. -> Retail $80K; Miles Co. -> Officials $15K
Dana (explain, sly): The hospital pays $120,000.
Dana: Miles Co. sells $80,000 to a retail pharmacy.
Dana: And keeps $15,000 as a kickback to the officials who approved it.

== Home | bag: "Premium insulin" / 1000 vials / $120K
Dr. Elaine (wave, sad): We’re giving patients the same medicine — just more expensive.
Dana: The cost doesn’t go to research or better care. It goes to profit.

== Board | percent: 30% kickback
Mr. Frank (point up, angry): Thirty percent of the price goes back to the buyers.
Dana: That’s a kickback. A secret share. Hidden in the contract.

== Timeline: January — Tender issued; March — Miles Co. wins; June — First delivery; September — Audit
Dr. Lee (think, worried): We didn’t notice the price jump until the audit.
Dana: The audit found the tender had no real competition. Only one bidder.

== List: Red flags; Only one supplier; Price three times higher; Kickback 30%; No quality test
Mr. Frank (point, angry): These are warning signs.
Dana (wave, happy): If you see only one name in a tender — that’s a red flag.

== Hospital | lesson: Watch the tender. Ask who wrote it. Compare prices.
Dana (explain, calm): Next time, ask: Is this really better medicine?
Dana (point, happy): Or is it just more expensive — with a secret profit for someone else?
